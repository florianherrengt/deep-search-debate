Come up with original product ideas based on the following concept. We want to create a **public internet artifact where companies, products, creators, or individuals pay to occupy a visible spot, but the best spots are contested rather than simply purchased once and kept forever.** The inspiration is the Million Dollar Homepage: people pay because they want their logo/product to visibly exist inside something that could itself become famous. But we want to add a **competitive ownership mechanic**. The important idea is: > **There is a shared, visually interesting world with scarce desirable positions. People pay not only to appear in it, but to compete with other people for control of those positions.** Examples of the kind of dynamic we mean: _ Someone owns a territory and another company can invade it. _ Someone occupies the #1 position and another company can challenge them for it. _ A company owns a building, object, tile, creature, planet, throne, etc., but somebody else can attempt to take it. _ Multiple companies compete over scarce locations whose ownership changes over time. _ Companies can expand, lose territory, defend assets, surround competitors, capture prestigious locations, or otherwise change the state of the shared artifact. These examples illustrate the mechanic. **Do not simply return variations of them.** ## What makes the concept work The product should combine five things. ### Paid placement Companies pay real money to put their: _ logo _ product _ website _ brand _ message into the experience. The monetization should be immediately understandable. ### Scarcity There must be genuinely desirable positions or assets. Owning one should mean something because not everyone can own it simultaneously. Scarcity could come from geography, rank, size, prominence, adjacency, uniqueness, history, limited supply, strategic importance, or another simple mechanic. ### Competition This is essential. Ownership should not necessarily be permanent. Other participants should be able to challenge, capture, displace, surround, outbid, attack, overtake, steal, or otherwise compete for desirable positions. Spending money can enable competition, but avoid a system where: > whoever spends the most money automatically wins. There should ideally be some additional mechanic that creates drama, strategy, community participation, timing, risk, or unpredictability. The changing ownership is part of what makes people return. ### Spectacle The shared artifact itself should be entertaining to observe even for someone who never buys anything. A visitor should be able to arrive and immediately see things happening such as: > “Stripe just took this from Revolut.” > “A tiny startup has somehow captured the most valuable position.” > “Three companies are currently fighting over this.” > “This company has occupied this position for 47 days.” The state of the product should constantly generate stories. ### Viral distribution Participants should naturally want to show other people what is happening. A company should have reasons to post things like: > “We're under attack. Help us defend our spot.” > “We just captured X.” > “We're trying to take the #1 position.” > “Come help us beat Y.” This means the companies paying for placement also become a distribution channel for the product. --- ## What we are looking for Find concepts where the **visual metaphor and competitive mechanic are exceptionally simple**. Someone should be able to see a screenshot and understand the product within seconds. Prefer ideas that feel like: > “Of course companies would fight over that.” The best concepts will create recognizable, scarce things that acquire prestige because other people also want them. They should also have a compelling visual end state as more participants join. We want something capable of becoming a strange internet landmark rather than merely another advertising platform. --- ## Important constraints The idea must be reasonably practical for a small team to build. Avoid concepts requiring: _ a full MMO _ sophisticated 3D worlds _ complicated economic simulations _ huge amounts of content _ millions of active players before they become interesting _ extensive moderation as the core mechanic The underlying rules should ideally fit in a few sentences. Avoid: _ normal advertising auctions _ generic directories _ sponsored leaderboards _ straightforward banner advertising _ Product Hunt clones _ generic virtual real estate _ NFT speculation _ concepts where the only competition is “pay more money” _ superficial visual reskins of Million Dollar Homepage The **competition itself should transform the public artifact**. --- ## Novelty is mandatory The concept must not already have been done in substantially the same form. Search for existing websites, experiments, games, advertising projects, crypto/NFT projects, art projects, startup experiments, old internet projects, and abandoned products that use substantially the same central mechanic. Do not count cosmetic differences as novelty. For example, these are probably the same underlying idea: _ companies buy countries and invade each other _ brands buy hexagons on a world map and capture neighbouring hexagons _ advertisers purchase regions of a map and fight over them Changing the graphics does not create a new concept. Reject ideas when a substantially similar implementation already exists. It is fine if individual ingredients have existed before. What matters is whether the **central combination of paid placement + scarce position + competitive ownership + public spectacle** is genuinely differentiated. The target is: > **A new kind of competitive public advertising surface where companies pay for visibility, scarce positions acquire status, participants fight over them, and those fights make the surface itself worth visiting.**
